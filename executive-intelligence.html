01The distinction

The intelligence above the stack.
Context, command, continuity.

A tool knows the prompt in front of it. Sky is designed to know the approved context around it: what the owner is trying to achieve, how the business operates, where authority sits and which intelligence is right for the task.

That is meta-intelligence: not one model claiming to do everything, but a private command architecture capable of selecting, coordinating and governing many forms of intelligence as one coherent system.

Assistant

Responds to a request.

Useful in the moment, but usually bounded by a conversation, a single interface and whatever context the user supplies.

Sky maintains approved operating context across the work.
Model

Provides one intelligence.

Different models have different strengths, costs, latency, privacy characteristics and deployment options.

Sky routes each job to the intelligence that fits its requirements.
Automation

Runs a defined sequence.

Automation moves a task. It does not automatically understand owner priorities, ambiguity or the weight of a decision.

Sky coordinates work inside explicit judgement and authority boundaries.

What is SKY Intelligence?

Sky is a private meta-intelligence architecture designed around an owner or leadership team. It maintains approved business context, coordinates suitable models and tools, prepares decisions and executes only inside defined authority boundaries.

How is SKY Intelligence structured?

Sky is the coordination and command layer. It brings approved context, interfaces, models, specialist agents and business systems into one governed operating architecture, selecting the right capability for each job.

Does Sky replace our current software?

Usually not. The architecture is designed to work across approved systems already in use, adding context, orchestration and control rather than forcing another disconnected system of record.

Which models and tools does Sky use?

There is no mandatory model stack. Technologies are evaluated against capability, privacy, latency, reliability, cost, deployment constraints and the client’s existing environment. The representative ecosystem above is a field of options, not a partnership list.

How is sensitive business information controlled?

The deployment defines data boundaries, access rules, infrastructure choices, retention requirements and approval paths before integration. The appropriate pattern depends on the client’s risk and regulatory context.

How much autonomy does Sky have?

Only the autonomy explicitly designed and approved for a bounded use case. Reading, preparing, recommending and acting are separate permissions. Consequential decisions remain with named human authorities.

What does a first engagement look like?

Start with one valuable operating priority. AIKING maps the context, systems, economics and authority boundary, then recommends whether a Sky capability is justified and what a controlled first deployment should include.
